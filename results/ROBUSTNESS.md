The router itself is the same machine code at every N: 91 instructions, 0 conditional branches, 0 memory operations - noise never reaches it; its input is one 44-bit word. What grows with N is only the state the body reads from: by position it is flat, by search it would be linear in N - that cost belongs to the body, not the router.

## 2. Other encodings of the same group

20,000 scenarios each, five worked examples with independent cases (the span is full).

| encoding | right | refused | **wrong** |
|---|---|---|---|
| codes carried in IEEE 754 float32 mantissas | 100.00% | 0.00% | **0.00%** |
| relabellings over the field GF(16), x -> a*x + b | 100.00% | 0.00% | **0.00%** |
| contrast: random permutations of the 16 codes (not affine) | 22.99% | 38.44% | **38.57%** |

Any representation that maps one-to-one onto the 16 codes and keeps the relabellings affine works unchanged - floats are decoded by bit extraction, GF(16)'s affine maps are a subset of GF(2)^4's. What the router cannot do is learn a relationship that is not affine. Five examples with independent cases fit at most one affine map: when the actions happen to fit one, the router answers - wrong wherever the permutation leaves it - and when they fit none, it refuses (G3). Refusal protects against too little evidence and against contradiction; it cannot detect a non-affine truth that happens to fit five points.
