# A whole domain, taught - code mutation

Generated by `neo4d/domain_mutation.py`. **The domain**: the standard mutation operators (ROR, AOR, ASR, LCR, BCR, NEG, CRP, BRK, RET - the operator sets of mutmut, PIT and Major) applied to every code line of the Python standard library on this machine: **179,243 lines in 187 modules, 210,078 mutants**. A reference mutator is the teacher and the ground truth.

**The experiment**: a quarter of the modules are held out. The swarm is taught k lines from the rest, each with all its mutants; every mutant is an event for its neurons, and the RELATE law (`neo4d/kernels/relate.c`, unchanged from the bug runs) sets every neuron's strength. Measured on 2,000 lines of the **held-out modules it never saw**, 3 different splits averaged:

- **coverage**: the share of their real mutants the active neurons generate exactly - did it learn the domain?
- **precision**: the share of generated candidates that are real mutants - does it mutate well, or spray?

| BCR | 2.0% | 20% | 40% | 77% | 95% |

| BRK | 0.5% | 0% | 33% | 100% | 100% |

**What this shows.**

- **It generalises to code it never saw, and expands with scale.** With anchored relations, coverage of the held-out modules' mutation domain grows from 52% at 100 taught lines to 87% at 1,000 and 96.0% at 10,000 - against a ceiling of 97.7% - while precision stays between 81% and 87% (the ceiling's own precision is 83%).
- **Fast or clean.** Literal edits and context-free relations learn faster (80%-84% at 100 lines) but spray: under 9% of what they propose is a real mutant. With literal edits beside the anchored relations the law starts at 66% precision and decays to 40% by 10,000 lines - each cheap literal is only slightly wrong (a `-` that is unary), the law spares cheap neurons by design, and the slight errors add up. Anchored relations alone learn slower and stay clean, and scale lets them catch up.
- **Memorisation does not transfer**: literal edits alone stop at 95% with 9% precision - a number or a name learned in one module is rarely the one another needs.
- **Where it stops**: `return <value> -> return None` (RET) at 47%. A long return value is an edit of more than 3 tokens, which no relation here represents.

**What this does not show.** The domain is defined by token-level operators, which the relation abstraction suits; a domain whose rules need wider context is harder. The cost budget is the body's: the bug runs' budget is in the third column, the domain's (at most 2 non-mutants per line) in the last two. The law is the same in every column. The teacher skips hexadecimal, binary and exponent literals and never touches comments or docstrings.
