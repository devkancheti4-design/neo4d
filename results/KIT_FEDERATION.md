# Kit 4 - federation: merging two numberings from anchors alone

Generated by `neo4d/kit_federation.py`. Party A knows the act for all 16 kinds; party B numbers the kinds its own way and knows nothing. They exchange only k anchors - (A's code, B's code) for shared faults. route4d5 learns the correspondence; B translates A's table. 50,000 trials per row, all 16 codes each.

| B's numbering | anchors | k | B knows, right | refused | **wrong** | exact right | exact wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| affine | random | 5 | 80.67% | 19.33% | **0.000%** | 80.77% | 0.000% |
| affine | span-aware | 5 | 100.00% | 0.00% | **0.000%** | 100.00% | 0.000% |
| arbitrary | span-aware | 5 | 22.98% | 38.68% | **38.331%** | 23.08% | 38.462% |
| arbitrary | span-aware | 6 | 2.44% | 94.39% | **3.162%** | 2.45% | 3.147% |
| arbitrary | span-aware | 7 | 0.29% | 99.42% | **0.292%** | 0.28% | 0.280% |
| arbitrary | span-aware | 8 | 0.03% | 99.95% | **0.020%** | 0.03% | 0.027% |

**What this shows.** When both numberings are affine, five span-aware anchors transfer A's entire table to B with nothing wrong; random anchors transfer the affine span of what was shared and refuse the rest. When B's numbering is arbitrary, the same router, from five anchors, answers 5 queries in 13 wrongly; each validation anchor cuts that about tenfold, exactly as the single-installation case.

**What this does not show - it is not zero-knowledge.** The anchors are code pairs in the clear, and the learned map reveals B's code for every one of A's 16 kinds to B, and to anyone who sees the anchors. Nothing here hides which faults a party has, proves a fix correct without showing it, or resists a party that lies about its anchors (a lie is a contradiction or a non-affine map: the router refuses or, from only five anchors, can be fooled - see the arbitrary rows). A real zero-knowledge exchange needs a cryptographic protocol, which no integer law here provides.
